Tips for Financial Cybersecurity

In today's world, technology is driving financial cybersecurity, and cybersecurity in general is one of the growing concerns.
Since our world relies on the internet for almost everything, we should learn to keep ourselves safe in this digital arena even before something bad happens.

Here are some simple textbook things to keep in mind to stay safe online:

1. Treat your personal information like valuable jewelry

Never hand it out to anyone: your Social Security number, credit card, bank card, or any of the accounts you have.
Any of these can be used to steal your money and do bad things in your name. So, every time you put your personal information into a website or app,
make sure you check:

1. The type of company you're dealing with
2. The type of product you're dealing with
3. The reviews to keep yourself safe

2. Protect your password

To protect your account, make sure you create a strong password and keep it safe.
The longer the password, the harder it is to crack.
Follow these guidelines:

1. Use at least 8 to 10 characters.
2. Mix letters, numbers, and special characters.
3. Try to be unpredictable: do not use your name, birthdate, or common words.
4. Don't use the same password multiple times.

3. Use your own device

Avoid using public computers or devices that aren't yours as much as possible. If you do use another device, make sure to delete your files,
clear cache, and clear history before you log out of the account.

4. Be safe using public Wi-Fi.

Free public hotspots are one of the main sources of attack, as they are unencrypted and easily accessible most of the time.
Don't connect to an untrusted Wi-Fi network. Wait until you can connect to a trusted, encrypted network to access your financial accounts.
When using Wi-Fi at home, secure your network with a strong password and strong encryption.

5. Enable multi-factor authentication.

After using a strong password, make sure you have enabled multi-factor authentication (MFA). It significantly reduces the chance of
a cybercriminal taking over your account, offering much stronger protection than single-factor authentication alone by adding another layer of security.

What are the signs of account takeover?

How to know if your account is already compromised(Though it will be less likely if you
do follow all these above-mentioned methods diligently, it is still not impossible.):

1. Missing funds or security: unfamiliar or unauthorized transactions, money movement, or deposits
2. Unexpected notifications indicating a change to your account that you didn't request
3. Unexpected loss of your mobile phone service, especially if SMS is used for MFA to your account
4. Missing account statements, confirmations, or other financial documents
5. Unfamiliar accounts or creditors on your account's list
6. Funds that have been frozen by your financial institution
